## About the deck

This deck works with Anki, which is an app/platform for studying flashcards. The two main concepts of Anki are active recall and spaced repetition. Active recall means each flashcard actively tests you on something (and you answer how well you got it). Spaced repetition means you review the cards at intervals determined by your answers. So, harder cards you see more often, and easier cards less often.

The goal of this style of language learning is, initially at least, recognition. Each card introduces one new piece of information at a time, and your goal is to understand the sentence. You rate yourself on how well you understood, and Anki manages when you review each card. Like this, with each card you will unlock the different words and grammar to start to understand the language. I think it's important to learn how to pronounce the cards, but being able to remember how to speak these sentences is not the initial goal.

A way to develop speaking ability in conjunction with working through this deck, is through exposure to the language (in-person, books, audio/video etc). Over time you will be exposed to the language in many different contexts and develop a sense of how to speak it.

- This deck helps unlock the ability to understand the language, to recognise what you're hearing/reading.
- Exposure to language puts it all in context, and from there you can start to speak.

But between these there's a gap, and it takes time and exposure to the language to close it. It's also totally normal to be able to understand a lot more than you can speak - it's the same in our native languages, especially when we were learning them. A way to start bridging this gap is to say the audio that is speak along the audio that is on the cards.


Anki rewards a slow and steady game, and to get the most out of this you want to try to make a daily habit of doing your reviews.

I would recommend only learning 5 new cards each day to start with. 5 might not seem like much, but you will soon be reviewing cards too, and they can add up. You can change the number of new cards each day in the deck options to suit where you're at.

## Technical stuff

At the moment the deck only has 29 cards, but as I check more, there will soon be a few hundred. It's still a work in progress, so there might be some things that don't make sense. Please let me know.

The sentences and definitions in this deck have come from the following resources:

- Jenny Green, *A Learner's Guide to Eastern and Central Arrernte* (IAD Press), with its audio CD
- *Learner's Wordlist of Eastern & Central Arrernte* (ECALL online dictionary)
- *Eastern and Central Arrernte to English Dictionary* (IAD Press)
- IAD Press Eastern & Central Arrernte picture dictionary
- *Thirty Phrases That Everyone Living in Mparntwe Should Know*
- David Wilkins, *Mparntwe Arrernte (Aranda): Studies in the structure and semantics of grammar* (1989)
- *Angkentye Yerrtye Ileme*, Arrernte street signs, Mparntwe

The sentences (text and audio) are as they were in the source, they haven't been modified. Some definitions have been reworded for brevity.

## Getting started

- **Android:** install [AnkiDroid](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.ichi2.anki) (free), download the deck and open the file.
- **Computer:** install [Anki](https://apps.ankiweb.net/) (free), download the deck and open the file.
- **iPhone/iPad:** the app, [AnkiMobile](https://apps.apple.com/app/ankimobile-flashcards/id373493387), is paid. For free, use [AnkiWeb](https://ankiweb.net) in the browser (iOS or desktop): open the deck once in Anki on a computer, then press Sync (AnkiWeb can't import .apkg files).

If Anki asks to import the deck’s options, say yes: that sets 5 new cards a day. If it doesn’t ask, change it in the deck options (Anki’s default of 20 is too many for this deck). 5 new cards a day doesn't sound like much, but once you start doing reviews, they can add up. See how you go, you can increase/decrease if you wish.

## About the deck

This deck works with Anki, which is an app/platform for studying flashcards. The two main concepts of Anki are active recall and spaced repetition. Active recall means each flashcard actively tests you on something (and you answer how well you got it). Spaced repetition means you review the cards at intervals determined by your answers. So, harder cards you see more often, and easier cards less often.

The goal of this style of language learning is, initially at least, recognition. Each card has a sentence that introduces one new piece of information at a time, and your goal is to understand the sentence. You rate yourself on how well you understood, and Anki manages when you review each card. Card by card, you will unlock the words and grammar to start to understand the language. It's set out to be a bit of a puzzle - each card only defines the one new piece of information (there's no full translation), so it's up to you to fill in the rest from what you've already learnt.

I think it's important to learn how to pronounce the cards, but being able to remember how to speak these sentences is not the initial goal. Alongside this deck, the way to develop speaking is exposure to the language (in-person, books, audio/video etc). Over time you will meet the language in many different contexts and develop a sense of how to speak it.

- This deck helps unlock the ability to understand the language, to recognise what you're hearing/reading.
- Exposure to language puts it all in context, and from there you can start to speak.

But between these there's a gap, and it takes time to close it. It's also totally normal to be able to understand a lot more than you can speak - it's the same in our native languages, especially when we were learning them. A way to start bridging this gap is to speak along with the audio on the cards and any other material you come across.

Anki rewards a slow and steady game, and to get the most out of this you want to try to make a daily habit of doing reviews. If you miss a day of reviews they'll still be waiting for you there the next day, but you'll be less likely to remember them since more time has passed.

## Sources

The sentences and definitions in this deck have come from the following resources:

- Jenny Green, [*A Learner's Guide to Eastern and Central Arrernte*](https://callprojects.org.au/languages/arrernte) (IAD Press), with its audio CD
- [*Learner's Wordlist of Eastern & Central Arrernte*](https://arrernte-angkentye.online/ecall/default.html) (ECALL online dictionary)
- [*Eastern and Central Arrernte to English Dictionary*](https://www.iadpress.com.au/products/eastern-and-central-arrernte-to-english-dictionary-revised-edition) (IAD Press)
- [IAD Press Eastern & Central Arrernte picture dictionary](https://iadpd.com.au/ecarrernte_category/ecarrernte/)
- [*Thirty Phrases That Everyone Living in Mparntwe Should Know*](https://soundcloud.com/watch-this-space-ari/sets/thirty-phrases-that-everyone)
- David Wilkins, [*Mparntwe Arrernte (Aranda): Studies in the structure and semantics of grammar*](https://openresearch-repository.anu.edu.au/handle/1885/9908) (1989)
- [*Angkentye Yerrtye Ileme*](https://www.angkentye-yerrtye-ilemele-mpwarele.com/), Arrernte street signs, Mparntwe

The sentences (text and audio) are exactly as they appear in the sources; some definitions have been reworded for brevity.
